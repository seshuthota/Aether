# P5 baseline: TFLOP/s per stage at 2048 (2026-11-15)

Gemma 4 E2B Q4_0, Adreno 830, 2048 tokens, batch 256, `profile_gemma_pp`
one pass. Times from the P3 pass at 38 °C / 2.40-2.44 GHz where it has the
stage (`p3-profile-2026-11-15.txt`); `q/k/v`, `wo` and the element-wise
stages from a full-stage pass at 45 °C / 1.99-2.40 GHz
(`p3-2048-full-stage-2026-11-15.txt`), which is ~5 % slower overall
(167.3 against 173.1 tok/s). FLOPs = 2 × MACs, the `vk_gemm` convention.
The "isolated" column is `bench/examples/vk_gemm.rs 256 gemma` on the same
phone, 39.2-39.6 °C (`vk-gemm-layout-2026-11-14.txt`), i.e. what the
shader does on one product with no model around it.

The attention FLOP estimate: `4 · hs · heads · Σ_t visible(t)` a layer, with
Σt = 917,760 for a 512-window layer at 2048 and 2,098,176 for a full one
(32 sliding × 8 heads × hs 256, 3 full × 8 × 512). It is an estimate from
the shapes, not a counter; the attention is not a dense GEMM.

## What this says for P5

- **The products are already at 71–99 % of the shader's own isolated
  rate.** Re-tiling the model would not find 2×: the current
  `q4_0_gemm_q8` tops out at ~1.4-1.5 TFLOP/s on these shapes (1.49
  TFLOP/s at 128 and 256 tokens alike, so it is not the token count). A 2×
  on the products means a different product shader, not different
  dispatch sizes.
- **The one cheap product win is the per-layer batch projection** at
  0.254 TFLOP/s: it is the f32 `qx_gemm` (Q8_0 dequantized to f32). The
  tuned path's `qx_gemm_q8` (int8 x, Q8_0 weights) is the same layout
  change the layer matrices already use; at the products' rate it would
  cut 222 ms to ~40-80 ms.
- **Attention is 44 % of the GPU stages and is overhead-bound**, not
  FLOP-bound: ~0.07 TFLOP/s against the products' 1.2-1.5. Its two costs
  are the 4-position tile barriers (a 512-position scan is 128 tiles × 2
  barriers a workgroup) and the serial online-softmax chain per pair.
  `SUB = 8` (8 positions a tile, 16 KiB of shared memory at hs 512) halves
  the barrier count without changing the pair mapping; a 512-thread
  workgroup at hs 512 would give TQ = 2 (the earlier 32-values/thread
  attempt kept 256 threads and lost to registers). These are the two
  experiments before P4.
- **The 2048 target needs both.** Products 5.79 s + attention 4.70 s +
  other 0.75 s = 11.24 s today; 400 tok/s is 5.12 s. Even a 2× product
  shader leaves ~7 s. Attention has to come down to ~1.5-2 s as well.

## The sliding window is skipped, and it shows

`attention_flash_gemma.comp` starts a workgroup's scan at
`first = pos + tok0 + 1 - window` (the oldest token in the workgroup) and
ends at `last = pos + end - 1`; nothing before the union of the group's
windows is staged, and `tj + window <= at` skips the positions a later
token cannot see. The union costs at most `TQ - 1` extra positions a
workgroup (1 at hs 256's TQ = 2, 0 at hs 512's TQ = 1). The
`gemma_window_8x1x256_p600_t33_w320` golden is the straddling case
(`first = 281 > 0`, a 33-token group); `p1500_t64_w512` and `p0_t4_w512`
cover the other edges.

The measured growth agrees: attention is 478 ms at 512 and 4696 ms at
2048. Without the window it would scale with positions (16× for 4× the
tokens); with it, the 32 sliding layers keep 512 positions while the 3
full layers do not, and the total is 9.8×.
