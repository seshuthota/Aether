Power and heat

A phone has no fan, and the battery is the power supply you can read. The fast configuration is often not the cheap one. Phase 1’s job is to make joules a measured number, not a vibe.

The definitions

Power is energy per time. Energy is what the battery spent. For a benchmark:

energy (joules) = average power (watts) × time (seconds).

At the battery, power is voltage times current. Read both. Do not assume a fixed voltage. Battery voltage drops as the charge runs down and sags further under load, so a current-only estimate multiplied by a nominal voltage is off by an amount that changes from run to run. Sample both, at the same moments.

The numbers you actually want, once a model exists, are joules per token and joules per GB read. Phase 1 can only produce the second. Joules per GB on a prime core versus a performance core is the comparison that tells you whether the faster core is the one you want for a long session. Faster and cheaper are different questions. Collect both.

The files

The plan points at /sys/class/power_supply/battery/:

FileUsual meaningCatch
current_nowInstantaneous currentUnits are often microamps, not amps. The sign flips by device: charging can be positive and discharging negative, or the reverse. Read the sibling current_avg if it exists, and a short idle sample, before you trust a sign.
voltage_nowBattery voltageOften microvolts. Divide consciously. Write the unit you assumed in the results file.
statusCharging, discharging, fullA run on the charger is a different thermal and electrical condition. The plan says unplug for energy numbers. A plugged-in phone may also hide discharge current.

These paths are conventional Android. Phase 0 did not open them. The first implementation step is to adb shell ls the directory and read one sample by hand, including the units, before any background thread exists. If this phone names the gauge differently, the code follows the phone, not the plan’s example path.

Frequency and temperature sit elsewhere:

  • /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpuN/cpufreq/scaling_cur_freq — the clock right now, in kHz.
  • /sys/class/thermal/ — thermal zones. Names vary. Note which zone you logged. A battery sensor and a CPU sensor are not interchangeable.

How to sample

A benchmark that reads the battery once at the start and once at the end misses the shape. Sample on a background thread, a few times a second, while the timed work runs.

Average power over the timed window, times the timed duration, is the energy of the run. Do not include the warm-up in the energy if you did not include it in the time, or the joules and the seconds describe different intervals.

The sensor is the whole phone: display, modem, OS, your process. You cannot get “joules of the kernel only” from the battery. You can get “joules of the phone while this kernel ran, screen in a known state, radio as quiet as you can make it, unplugged.” That is the number to compare across core sets. A difference of a few percent is the sensor and the OS. A difference of two times is the core set.

Heat is a clock change

The governor lowers scaling_cur_freq as the SoC heats up. Performance in the first 10 seconds is not performance after 5 minutes. The plan asks for a long run that logs frequency and temperature and notes how far bandwidth drops.

What you are looking for is not a single throttled GB/s. It is the curve: how many seconds until the clocks step down, which cores step down, and whether bandwidth follows the clock (compute-bound or latency-bound) or stays flat (already waiting on a bus that did not change). A streaming DRAM read may throttle less than a hot NEON kernel, or the memory controller itself may be on the same thermal budget. The log answers it. A prediction will not.

Screen on is a different experiment from screen off. The display is a large, steady load, and it changes thermals. Pick one for the table, and say which.

What to write down

Every results row, from this phase onward:

  • commit, release build,
  • cores pinned, and that the pin succeeded,
  • screen, charger, starting temperature,
  • median time and the GB/s convention (which bytes you counted),
  • average current, average voltage, the units you divided by, joules,
  • frequency range observed during the window.

A table of GB/s with none of that is not the Phase 1 deliverable. The explanation of why prime and performance differ, or do not, is the deliverable.

Check yourself

Why multiply current by voltage instead of using current alone?

Power is their product. Voltage sags under load. Two runs with the same current and different voltages did not spend the same energy. The files are often micro-units; forgetting the 106 makes the joules nonsense, so write the conversion next to the number.

Why unplug?

A charging phone is not discharging the battery at the rate your process costs, and the current sign or magnitude may describe the charger. Thermals are also different. Energy numbers in the plan are unplugged numbers.

The first 10 seconds sustain 25 GB/s and minute 5 sustains 15. Which one goes in the table?

Both, labeled. The first is the optimistic roof for a short benchmark. The second is what a conversation feels like. Comparing a kernel’s 14 GB/s against the optimistic 25 and calling it 56% efficient is the wrong fraction if the phone cannot hold 25.
