After Phase 8 — faster decoding, the GPU, the NPU and a hybrid model

By Phase 8 the engine ran in an app at close to the phone’s memory limit. The work since asks two questions. Can one read of the weights produce more than one token? And what is the phone’s GPU good for, when it reads the same memory as the CPU? Two more pieces came after: the phone’s NPU, a Hexagon DSP running our own C code, and LFM3-1.7B, a hybrid model whose layers are not all attention.

Why go further than the plan? What was built? What are the headline numbers? Which page should I read first? What is still open? Which files hold the code?

Why go further than the plan?

The plan’s section 13 lists later work: K-quant files, speculative decoding, Vulkan compute on the GPU, and the NPU. A hybrid model came later, with its own plan (plans/lfm3.md). Decode had reached about 180 tokens per second for Qwen2.5-0.5B Q4_0, about 50 GB/s of weights calculated, close to the memory’s 61–67 GB/s. A faster kernel could not give much more. Getting several tokens per weight read could, and the GPU might do the same work for less energy.

Takeaway: once decode is near the memory limit, the next gains come from more tokens per read and from cheaper hardware, not faster kernels.

What was built?

  • Q4_K_M files. llama.cpp’s most common mix (Q5_0, Q4_K and Q6_K blocks) loads and runs, with perplexity checked against llama.cpp (results/phase-6.md, “Q4_K_M files”). Page: Q4_K_M files.
  • Speculative decoding. Draft tokens, copied from earlier text (prompt lookup) or proposed by a smaller model, checked in one batched pass. CLI --lookup K, --draft PATH; the app’s “lookup” checkbox.
  • Vulkan compute on the Adreno GPU. A Q4_0 matrix-vector and matrix-matrix shader, then a whole decode step, then the prompt: on the CPU with the KV cache handed to the GPU, or batched on the GPU. CLI --gpu; the app’s “GPU” checkbox.
  • The NPU (Hexagon DSP). Our own C code, unsigned, called over FastRPC: a whole decode step per call, the weights in on-chip memory (VTCM), and prompts in passes on the matrix unit (HMX). Built with --features hexagon; the app’s CPU / GPU / DSP selector. Source: dsp/aether_dsp.c.
  • LFM3-1.7B, a hybrid model. Short-convolution layers and attention layers in one stack, with a sliding window in some attention layers. Its speculative decoding rolls the convolution state back. Page: LFM3-1.7B.

Takeaway: each addition was checked against an outside reference before any speed was measured.

Which page should I read first?

  1. Several tokens per weight read: drafts, the rule that keeps them, lookup against a draft model, and speculative sampling.
  2. How the GPU runs our code: lanes, workgroups, Vulkan, and how a matrix-vector product is split across lanes.
  3. Decoding on the GPU: the whole step, attention in chunks, clock swings, energy, and where the time goes.
  4. Who reads the prompt?: the KV-cache handoff from CPU to GPU, a misleading measurement, and a batched GPU pass.
  5. Q4_K_M files: llama.cpp’s most common mix, K-quant super-blocks, accuracy against llama.cpp, and two speed fixes.

Then the GPU’s int8 products, and why flash attention changed the prompt pass: why round the activations to 8 bits on the GPU, and flash attention: was it the kernel or the batch?. The NPU has four pages: what the NPU is, and how our code gets on it, why decode is one call per token, how the NPU reads a prompt, and how attention streams through VTCM. CPU, GPU or NPU compares every engine on this phone, and LFM3-1.7B is the hybrid model.

Takeaway: the speculative and Q4_K_M pages stand alone; the GPU pages build on each other.

What is still open?

  • GPU: the newest GPU work is timed on the phone (results/phase-l3.md, steps 7 and 21 to 28). Still open: splitting a short token group’s positions across workgroups, the next step after 256-token passes (step 27, F6b).
  • L.2, an importance-weighted quantizer of our own, only if a model needs it.
  • NPU: built and measured (results/phase-l5.md). Next for decode: attention over the whole cache in vectors of positions, since decode slows as the position grows (step 4b, 2026-10-30).
  • K-quant decode (branch kquant-decode, K0–K5) is in progress and has no phone numbers yet. For comparison, LFM3’s Q4_K_M decode measured 26.1 tok/s against llama.cpp’s 44.3 (results/phase-lfm3.md A7, 2026-11-03).
