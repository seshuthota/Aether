# Core Principles — Expanded

## Animation Is the Explanation
In *Linear Transformations*, the grid morphs in real time as the narrator says “squishing and stretching space.” The motion itself conveys linearity (straight lines stay straight, origin fixed) before any matrix is shown.

In *Fourier Transform*, the graph wraps around a circle while the words describe the wrapping. The “whoop” of alignment is timed with the visual snap.

**Rule**: If a viewer can pause at any moment and redraw the current state of the diagram from memory, the animation is doing its job.

## Intuition Before Formalism
*Neural Network* begins with “a thing that holds a number between 0 and 1” and glowing pixels. The weighted sum and activation function appear only after the layered structure is visually clear.

*Vectors* starts with arrows and three perspectives; the formal definition is deferred until later in the series.

**Rule**: Introduce the name or formula only after a concrete picture has already made the idea feel inevitable.

## Progressive Revelation
Layers of a neural net appear one after another. Basis vectors are tracked individually before their coordinates are packaged into a matrix. Cache-line edges or load rectangles should enter when first mentioned, not as a finished diagram.

**Rule**: Never dump a complete complex figure. Use lagged starts so the viewer’s mental model grows at the same speed as the picture.

## Tight Synchrony and Pacing
Narration describes the motion while it happens. After a key reveal there is a short hold. Silence is used deliberately. Pacing stays calm; speed increases only after the core idea is solid.

**Rule**: The voice and the picture must feel like one stream, not a voiceover laid on top of finished frames.
